Training Arc

Android · Kotlin v1.0.0 · closed testing on Google Play

Your gym log, turned into an RPG. Every set earns XP.

An Android workout logger where every set earns XP, every session raises six attributes and every week in a row feeds your streak. It has 883 exercises, 8 themed programmes, personal records, a muscle map and statistics. It works offline with no account, in Spanish and English.

01 The problem

Workout logging apps are effective but dry, and many people struggle to stay consistent. Turning progress into a character who levels up gives one more reason to go back to the gym.

02 A look at the code

03 What I built

  • Kotlin and Jetpack Compose with a single Activity, Navigation Compose, Room for workouts and routines and DataStore for settings and the hero.
  • Pure, deterministic Kotlin RPG engine: XP per set by exercise type, bonuses for records, class and streak, six attributes and ranks from E to SS. Totals can always be rebuilt from the stored workouts.
  • Live workout with last session's values, set types and a rest timer that keeps running in the notification while the phone is locked.
  • Personal records detected on finish (Epley 1RM estimate), 14 achievements, a weekly goal, progress charts and body weight.
  • Custom vector muscle map, front and back, used on exercise pages, results and as a weekly heat map.
  • 883 exercises from Free Exercise DB (public domain) prepared with a Python script, with instructions translated on device with ML Kit.
  • No backend and no accounts: exportable backups, offline images and cache and data deletion. The earlier Flutter and FastAPI prototype is kept under a tag.
  • 34 unit tests for the RPG engine, streaks, achievements, progress, the catalogue and formatting.

04 Decisions and why

  1. Progress is computed, not stored. The RPG engine is a pure function, so level and attributes can always be rebuilt from the workouts: a rule change or a bug never leaves corrupted progress.
  2. Absurd values (600 kg, 500 reps) are clamped rather than rejected. A typo shouldn't lose a set, but it shouldn't hand out levels either.
  3. No backend: the Flutter and FastAPI prototype was replaced by a native app that keeps everything on the phone. With no accounts or server there are no running costs and no health data leaves the device.

Transparency: the status above reflects the project roadmap. The repository is private because the app is on Google Play; the code excerpt is verbatim and I can walk through the rest in an interview.

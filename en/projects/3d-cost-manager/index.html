3D Cost Manager

Android · Kotlin v1.0.0 · closed testing on Google Play

Know what each print really costs you and what to charge everywhere you sell.

An Android app that works out the real cost of each 3D-printed part (material, electricity, wear, your time and failed prints) and what to sell it for on each channel, fees and VAT included. It manages quotes and orders with PDFs and runs entirely on the phone, with no account or server, in 6 languages.

  • No account, works offline
  • Imports G-code and 3MF
  • PDF quotes
  • 6 languages

01 The problem

Almost every 3D printing calculator only counts filament. People selling on Etsy or Wallapop end up undercharging because they forget electricity, wear, their own time, failed prints and the channel's fee.

04 What I built

  • Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, Room and DataStore. Everything lives on the device, with no account or server.
  • Pure Kotlin calculation engine (CostEngine) with BigDecimal: scale 8 in the steps and 4 in the result. A failed plate repeats material, electricity, wear and prep, but not finishing or extras.
  • Price solved so that, after VAT and the channel's fee on the total, the target profit is left, with a channel comparison (Etsy, Amazon, eBay, Wallapop and your own shop).
  • G-code and 3MF import from Flash Studio, OrcaSlicer, Bambu Studio, PrusaSlicer and Cura: time, grams per filament and a picture of the part.
  • Quotes and orders with statuses, PDFs with the business logo, a monthly summary with a chart and CSV export.
  • Print timer with a home-screen widget, spool stock with low-stock alerts and Spain's daily electricity price (PVPC).
  • Six languages generated from a script that fails if a language is missing or has an extra key.
  • It started as a Spring Boot backend with JWT, PostgreSQL and AWS infrastructure as code (69 tests). It was archived once the app no longer needed a server and the engine was ported to Kotlin. The app has 37 tests.

05 Decisions and why

  1. From server to phone. The first version had a Spring Boot backend and AWS infrastructure. For a personal calculator, an account and a server were friction and cost with no upside, so everything moved to the device and the backend was archived in case sync is ever needed.
  2. BigDecimal with 8 internal decimals and HALF_UP rounding to 4 at the end. With money, double's floating-point errors end up as cents that don't add up.
  3. The price is solved instead of adding percentages, because marketplaces take their fee on the VAT-inclusive total and a plain margin leaves less profit than you think.
  4. Each quote line freezes its price and cost when added. If filament gets dearer tomorrow, a quote already sent doesn't change by itself; "Update prices" recalculates it on purpose.

Transparency: the status above reflects the repository README. Anything planned is labelled as such; the full code and documentation are on GitHub.
